01 · The Risk

Misclassification: when the contractor label isn't what protects you

Engaging talent as an independent contractor lowers the tax burden — but only holds up if it reflects genuine autonomy. Labor courts and tax authorities judge the relationship by its substance, not by the label on the contract.

Subordination and personal service

If the client sets working hours, gives direct day-to-day instructions, requires exclusivity, or treats the professional as part of the internal team, the relationship can be read as disguised employment — regardless of what the contract says.

Retroactive liability

If the structure is reclassified, the contracting company can be held liable for unpaid employer charges, severance, fines and interest — calculated over the entire contract period, not just from the date of the ruling.

Direct exposure for the end client

When the professional is engaged directly by the client with no intermediary, it's the client who answers to the worker and to the authorities — there's no third party contractually positioned between the parties.

Why this matters here Model 3 is attractive precisely because it lowers the tax load versus EOR — but that advantage only holds if the structure is designed, documented and monitored to preserve genuine autonomy. That design is exactly what Wide Brazil delivers.
02 · Our Role

Why Wide Brazil as intermediary changes the risk profile

Wide Brazil sits between the client and the professional — not as a formality, but as the piece that reduces the elements that characterize a direct employment relationship.

Breaking the direct link

The commercial contract is between the client and Wide Brazil (Uruguay entity) — not between the client and the professional. The professional delivers services through Wide, which removes the direct bilateral relationship that normally underpins a reclassification claim.

Contracts drafted to protect the client

Scope defined by deliverables (not by working hours), no direct hierarchical subordination, clear allocation of responsibilities, confidentiality and IP protection — designed to support the nature of a services engagement.

Ongoing compliance advisory across LATAM

Continuous monitoring of the rules in every country where Wide operates (Brazil, Uruguay, Chile, Argentina), with practical guidance on what can and can't be asked of the professional to keep the contractor structure defensible — and adjustments as legislation or case law evolves.

Wide as the management layer

Wide handles payment, a single invoice, and is the professional's formal point of contact — the client directs outcomes and deliverables, not day-to-day work instructions.
